External transaction evidence

Market evidence.
Without false comparability.

The portfolio now maintains a primary-source transaction evidence layer. These transactions demonstrate strategic buyer appetite across relevant technology categories, but they are not copied directly into portfolio asset values. Each observed target is materially larger, more mature or more commercialized than most portfolio assets.

What the evidence can prove

Strategic markets pay for software, IP and capability — but comparability still matters.

Recent primary-source transactions show substantial strategic consideration for enterprise AI, data, cloud infrastructure, identity security, engineering simulation, scientific R&D software, vertical workflow software and creator technology. The correct use is to calibrate sector demand and confidence, not to multiply an early-stage asset by a mature-company revenue multiple.

Observed buyer appetite

Large strategic acquirers continue to buy software and technology capabilities to accelerate time to market, enter adjacent categories and combine complementary IP.

Observed strategic premiums

Several mature software transactions in the evidence set imply deal-value/revenue ratios well above 1×, reflecting growth, recurring revenue, market position, IP, customer relationships and buyer synergies.

Direct application prohibited

The portfolio assets are generally smaller, earlier and frequently pre-revenue. Therefore observed transaction values and multiples are evidence of category economics, not direct pricing formulas.

Evidence first, price second

Transaction evidence currently changes confidence in the market lens. A direct market valuation requires subject-asset comparability in rights, maturity, revenue, growth, useful life, geography, date and transaction structure.

Framework discipline

Observed evidence changes confidence before it changes price.

WIPO and RICS both require judgement around comparability, uncertainty and subject-asset characteristics. OECD guidance additionally cautions that development cost often has little correlation with transfer value once an intangible has been created. The evidence layer therefore prevents two opposite mistakes: treating code cost as full value, or treating a billion-dollar acquisition as a direct comparable for an early-stage asset.

From market context to asset evidence

An acquisition headline is not an asset-level result.

The transaction register records cited announcements and source context. It is not a live deal-status feed; announcement consideration and closing status must be checked at the cited source before reuse.

Demand context

External transactions can indicate relevant buyer categories. They do not prove demand for this particular asset or establish its price.

Technical and scientific claims

Assess the claim, evidence scope, limitations and reproducibility privately where appropriate. No new technical performance or scientific validation is asserted by this website update.

Rights and commercial claims

Evaluate title, transferability and economic assumptions separately. A test result cannot settle ownership, licensing permissions or market value.
