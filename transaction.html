Professional engagement

Asset first.
Then diligence.

The transaction unit is the individual standalone IP asset. Classification families may help a counterparty discover related projects, but a commercial discussion should identify the exact TA-IP asset or assets being considered.

Potential structures

Four principal transaction routes.

No structure is implied by this memorandum. Scope, ownership, licence rights, deliverables and consideration are established only through definitive agreements that identify the relevant asset or assets.

01

Single-asset acquisition

Purchase of one defined standalone software/IP asset with its own transaction perimeter.

02

Multi-asset acquisition

Purchase of two or more standalone assets, with each asset separately identified in the transaction scope.

03

Exclusive / non-exclusive licensing

Asset-specific rights structured by field of use, market, geography, exclusivity, term or other negotiated restrictions.

04

Technology transfer / strategic integration

A negotiated transfer or integration of the named asset or assets without extending rights to unrelated projects.

Indicative process

A five-gate route from asset selection to definitive agreement.

The process is structured to preserve asset-level boundaries and avoid exposing sensitive material before a counterparty and purpose are established.

01

Identify

Name the exact project and TA-IP reference, or list every asset if more than one is being considered.

02

Qualify

Define whether the interest is acquisition, licensing, technology transfer, strategic integration or another commercial structure.

03

Confidentiality

Agree the communication channel, counterparty identity, disclosure perimeter and confidentiality framework.

04

Diligence

Review technical, provenance, licensing, evidence, build and commercial material separately for each included asset.

05

Terms & closing

Negotiate consideration, rights, representations, transfer mechanics and definitive documentation for the identified asset set.

Asset-level diligence

Every project carries its own diligence perimeter.

Even when multiple assets are negotiated together, each asset should be evaluated on its own source, provenance, rights, maturity, dependencies and transferability.

Technical state

Repository scope, architecture, build path, interfaces, runtime/deployment model, current maturity and known limitations.

Evidence & verification

Tests, validation outputs, benchmark material, reproducibility information and any scientific or engineering evidence relevant to claims.

Provenance & ownership

Repository history, contribution provenance, ownership assertions and the defined asset boundary.

Dependencies & licences

Third-party software, models, datasets, libraries, external services, licence obligations and transfer restrictions where applicable.

Commercial perimeter

Proposed rights, exclusivity, territory, field of use, support assumptions, deliverables and retained rights for that asset.

Transfer readiness

Code/material delivery, documentation, credentials or secrets exclusion, transition requirements and acceptance criteria.
