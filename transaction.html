A serious discussion starts with a named asset or cluster and a defined transaction objective. Source-level access and sensitive technical material are not public browsing material; they belong inside a scoped diligence process with an identified counterparty.

Potential structures

Four principal transaction routes.

No structure is implied by this memorandum. Scope, ownership, licence rights, deliverables and consideration are established only through definitive agreements.

01

Selected asset acquisition

Purchase of one or more defined software/IP assets or a thematic cluster.

02

Whole-portfolio acquisition

A broader acquisition where a counterparty has a strategic rationale for the active portfolio as a combined asset package.

03

Exclusive / non-exclusive licensing

Rights structured by field of use, market, geography, exclusivity, term or other negotiated restrictions.

04

Technology transfer / strategic integration

A negotiated transfer or integration perimeter without requiring acquisition of unrelated assets.

Indicative process

A five-gate route from first contact to definitive agreement.

The process is intentionally structured to avoid exposing sensitive technical information before a counterparty and commercial purpose are established.

01

Identify

Name the asset, cluster or portfolio scope and the professional organisation or role behind the inquiry.

02

Qualify

Define whether the interest is acquisition, licensing, technology transfer, strategic integration or another commercial structure.

03

Confidentiality

Agree the communication channel, counterparty identity, disclosure perimeter and confidentiality framework.

04

Diligence

Review the agreed technical, provenance, licensing, evidence, build and commercial material for the selected perimeter.

05

Terms & closing

Negotiate consideration, rights, representations, transfer mechanics and definitive documentation.

Diligence scope

What a professional counterparty can ask to evaluate.

The exact package depends on the asset and transaction. The categories below define the intended diligence perimeter; they do not represent that every document exists in identical form for every asset.

Technical state

Repository scope, architecture, build path, interfaces, runtime/deployment model, current maturity and known limitations.

Evidence & verification

Tests, validation outputs, benchmark material, reproducibility information and any scientific or engineering evidence relevant to claims.

Provenance & ownership

Repository history, contribution provenance, ownership assertions and the defined asset boundary.

Dependencies & licences

Third-party software, models, datasets, libraries, external services, licence obligations and transfer restrictions where applicable.

Commercial perimeter

Proposed rights, exclusivity, territory, field of use, support assumptions, deliverables and any retained rights.

Transfer readiness

Code/material delivery, documentation, credentials or secrets exclusion, transition requirements and acceptance criteria.
