Five-lens architecture

A conventional valuation process requires more than development cost.

WIPO identifies cost, market and income approaches and also discusses real options and Monte Carlo techniques for uncertain technology. RICS requires legal, functional, market and economic characteristics to be considered when applying IP valuation methods.

01

Replacement Cost

Existing estimated recreation cost for comparable software, engineering work product and present project state.

02

Market / Comparable

The current figure is a modelled proxy. Verified transactions must be normalized for rights, maturity, scale, revenue, geography, transaction date and buyer context before they can support a subject-asset market valuation.

03

Income / Licensing

The current figure is a monetization proxy. A full income valuation requires defendable revenue, margin, royalty, useful-life, remaining-development-cost and discount-rate inputs.

04

Probability-adjusted Strategic Value

Each asset is adjusted for technical completion, commercialization probability, rights/transfer diligence reserve, time/risk and sector context.

05

Seller Asking Price

The seller-side asking reference remains independent from the analytical economic-value model and may be deliberately conservative.

06

Triangulation

The preliminary economic reference combines replacement cost 25%, market proxy 20%, income/licensing proxy 20% and probability-adjusted strategic value 35%.

External market evidence

Primary transactions calibrate confidence — not copy prices.

IM20 introduces a primary-source evidence register spanning enterprise AI, cloud infrastructure, security, industrial simulation, scientific software, vertical workflow software and creator technology. These deals demonstrate that strategic buyers pay substantial consideration for software and IP capability, but the observed targets are larger, more mature and generally revenue-generating.

Replacement-cost completeness

€60.150M remains a baseline until all cost components are audited.

A pure-code replacement estimate can understate the real cost of rebuilding an IP asset. IM20 therefore refuses to assume that the current baseline already captures every non-code component, while also preventing arbitrary automatic uplift.

Architecture & systems design

Architecture, interfaces, data models and technical decision work must be separately checked for inclusion.

Research & domain work

Scientific review, domain modelling, feasibility work and failed approaches may represent substantial recreation effort.

QA, validation & security

Testing, benchmarking, hardening, threat modelling and validation campaigns must be quantified where present.

Deployment & documentation

Packaging, operations, observability, installation, handover material and operator documentation are part of transferable work product.

IP, licence & provenance qualification

Dependency review, third-party rights, data/model rights and transferability analysis are necessary transaction costs and may affect value.

Productization & programme management

UX, commercial packaging, requirements, coordination, release management and quality gates can be material to rebuild economics.

No automatic uplift. The baseline is not multiplied by an arbitrary percentage. Each asset must be audited component by component so already-included work is not double counted. The completeness framework is stored in assets/replacement-cost-scope.json.

Probability architecture

Commercialization risk remains explicit.

A large theoretical opportunity is not treated the same as a smaller opportunity with a much higher probability of completion and adoption.

Technical completion probability

Research-stage 50% · Developed prototype 75% · Developed software 95%.

Commercialization probability

Specialist 25% · Moderate 40% · High 55% · Very High 70%.

Time / risk adjustment

Research-stage 70% · Developed prototype 85% · Developed software 95%.

Rights / transfer diligence reserve

85% is applied pending transaction-stage verification of ownership, third-party rights, licences, dependencies and transferability.

Model boundary. These probabilities and multipliers are explicit internal modelling assumptions; they are not prescribed by WIPO, IVSC, RICS, OECD, IFRS, ISO, EPO, JPO or USPTO. They should progressively be replaced by asset-specific evidence.

Next diligence layer

Move from sector evidence to subject-asset evidence.

External transaction anchors are now captured. The next value upgrade comes from matching each asset to narrower evidence rather than increasing portfolio-wide multipliers.

01

Replacement audit

Componentize current recreation cost and identify missing architecture, R&D, QA, security, documentation, deployment, compliance and IP-cleanup effort.

02

Narrow comparables

Match subject assets to transactions or licences with similar rights, maturity, sector, geography, useful life and date.

03

Royalty evidence

Introduce defensible royalty benchmarks and relief-from-royalty analysis where licensing is a realistic transaction route.

04

Buyer economics

Model revenues, margins, avoided internal R&D, time-to-market savings and integration synergies for a defined acquirer.

05

Evidence-weighted triangulation

Re-weight valuation methods per asset according to evidence quality instead of one universal portfolio weighting.
