Valuation decision · IM25

Value needs
project-specific evidence.

The re-evaluation does not establish a current monetary value for any project or for the portfolio as a whole. This is an evidence limitation, not a conclusion that the work has no value.

Why the previous model is not reconfirmed.

  1. All 79 previous market and income proxies and strategic calculations reproduce from the same replacement-cost anchor and generic factors.
  2. The model provides multiple scenarios of one cost assumption, not four independent observations of value.
  3. The reviewed materials do not establish component-level replacement estimates with rates, effort and obsolescence adjustments.
  4. No asset-specific comparable-rights transactions or reliable buyer cash-flow model are established in the reviewed material.
  5. Generic technical and commercial probabilities were assigned by broad labels; no asset-specific calibration supports their use as measured probabilities.
  6. A common weighting does not resolve missing evidence or the correlation between calculations.
  7. Portfolio addition requires separate rights, overlapping work and buyer synergy review; a sum of standalone references is not a portfolio sale value.

In the earlier model, replacement cost is multiplied by stage, potential, sector and route factors to generate the other indicators. With those factors fixed, changing the cost anchor by 10% changes every unrounded modelled value by 10%. Combining the results does not remove that common assumption.

The previous labels were not empirically calibrated completion or commercialisation probabilities. Version numbers, test counts and repository growth do not supply that calibration.

Method eligibility comes first.

Method selection follows the nature of the work and the evidence available. WIPO describes the distinct information needs of cost, market and income approaches. Its guidance does not validate these projects or prescribe the earlier multipliers. Read WIPO’s IP valuation guidance.

A usable valuation brief.

  1. Identify the exact project, version, rights and intended transaction date.
  2. Define the reusable work and intended buyer use, excluding unavailable or third-party material.
  3. Document effort, rates, remaining work and obsolescence for any cost approach.
  4. Provide relevant transaction terms or defensible cash flows for market or income approaches.
  5. Assess overlap, dependencies and buyer-specific benefits before any portfolio total.
  6. Document assumptions, sensitivity and the evidence supporting the selected method.
