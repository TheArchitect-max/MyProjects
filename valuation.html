Five-lens architecture

A conventional valuation process requires more than development cost.

WIPO identifies cost, market and income approaches and also discusses real options and Monte Carlo techniques for uncertain technology. RICS requires legal, functional, market and economic characteristics to be considered when applying IP valuation methods.

01

Replacement Cost

Existing estimated recreation cost for comparable software, engineering work product and present project state.

02

Market / Comparable

Current figure is a modelled proxy based on maturity, potential and sector context. Verified comparable transactions must replace or calibrate this proxy during diligence.

03

Income / Licensing

Current figure is a monetization proxy. A full income valuation requires defendable revenue, margin, royalty, useful-life, development-cost and discount-rate inputs.

04

Probability-adjusted Strategic Value

Each asset is adjusted for technical completion, commercialization probability, rights/transfer diligence reserve, time/risk and sector context.

05

Seller Asking Price

The seller-side asking reference remains independent from the analytical economic-value model and may be deliberately conservative.

06

Triangulation

The preliminary economic reference combines replacement cost 25%, market proxy 20%, income/licensing proxy 20% and probability-adjusted strategic value 35%.

Probability architecture

Commercialization risk is now explicit.

A large theoretical opportunity is not treated the same as a smaller opportunity with a much higher probability of completion and adoption.

Technical completion probability

Research-stage 50% · Developed prototype 75% · Developed software 95%.

Commercialization probability

Specialist 25% · Moderate 40% · High 55% · Very High 70%.

Time / risk adjustment

Research-stage 70% · Developed prototype 85% · Developed software 95%.

Rights / transfer diligence reserve

85% is applied pending transaction-stage verification of ownership, third-party rights, licences, dependencies and transferability.

Model boundary. These probabilities and multipliers are explicit internal modelling assumptions; they are not prescribed by WIPO, IVSC, RICS, OECD, IFRS, ISO, EPO, JPO or USPTO. Their purpose is to make uncertainty visible rather than hide it inside one portfolio multiplier.

Confidence by lens

Not every number has the same evidentiary strength.

The model explicitly separates observable/cost-anchored information from proxies that require external commercial evidence before they can support a formal market-value opinion.

Replacement cost · Medium confidence

Based on existing recreation-cost work but still subject to refinement for full architecture, research, QA, security, documentation, deployment, compliance and IP-cleanup costs.

Market proxy · Low confidence

Requires actual arm's-length comparable transactions, licensing deals and rights-perimeter normalization.

Income proxy · Low confidence

Requires buyer-specific cash flows or defensible licensing economics before it can become a conventional income-method result.

Strategic value · Modelled

Uses explicit probabilities and buyer-utility assumptions, but the final value will vary by acquirer, integration route and market access.

Institutional reference architecture

Nine reference layers inform terminology and method selection.

The framework is informed by WIPO, International Valuation Standards / IVSC, RICS, OECD intangibles and DEMPE principles, IFRS IAS 38, ISO 56005, EPO, JPO and USPTO material. None of those institutions has reviewed or endorsed a specific portfolio value.

Next diligence layer

Replace proxies with external evidence asset by asset.

The present model is deliberately designed so that stronger evidence can replace provisional inputs without changing the identity of the underlying TA-IP asset.

01

Comparable transactions

Collect and normalize software/IP assignments and licences by sector, maturity, rights scope and transaction date.

02

Royalty evidence

Introduce defensible royalty benchmarks and relief-from-royalty analysis for assets with realistic licensing routes.

03

Buyer economics

Model revenues, margins, avoided internal R&D, time-to-market savings and integration synergies for a defined buyer.

04

Risk and optionality

Refine probabilities, discount rates, useful life, development milestones and real-options value using asset-specific evidence.

05

Formal triangulation

Weight the most appropriate methods for the subject asset and transaction purpose rather than using one universal portfolio factor.
