Valuation architecture

Cost is the baseline.
Potential is separate.

The public asking references are intentionally conservative and should not be read as the full economic potential of the underlying IP. This page separates three different concepts: seller-side asking reference, recreation/replacement cost and modelled strategic potential.

Three valuation layers

Do not confuse price, cost and potential value.

WIPO explicitly distinguishes price from value and identifies cost, market and income approaches as principal IP valuation methods. The current portfolio asking level is positioned primarily as a conservative cost-anchored transaction reference, while strategic potential is shown separately.

01

Asking reference

€14.075M aggregate. A seller-side transaction reference across all 79 standalone assets. It is deliberately below the current aggregate recreation-cost baseline.

02

Replacement-cost baseline

€60.150M aggregate. A cost-approach reference for recreating comparable software, engineering work product and current project state.

03

Strategic potential expectation

€78.5M modelled base expectation. A portfolio-level scenario reference that incorporates maturity and existing commercial-potential classifications without inventing unsupported project revenue forecasts.

04

Scenario envelope

€47.1M–€138.3M. A deliberately wide low-to-high strategic-potential range reflecting uncertainty, development stage and buyer-specific commercialization optionality.

Conservative pricing position

The asking reference is materially below both cost and modelled potential.

This distinction is important commercially: a low asking price can reflect transaction strategy and development-stage risk without implying that the IP has only that amount of economic utility.

Potential model

A transparent portfolio-level bridge above replacement cost.

Because most assets do not yet have buyer-specific revenue histories, royalty benchmarks or reliable comparable transactions, a full income-method valuation would require assumptions that cannot currently be defended. The strategic-potential layer therefore uses explicit maturity and qualitative-potential factors rather than fabricated cash flows.

Commercial-potential factors

Base scenario: Specialist 0.75× · Moderate 1.00× · High 1.50× · Very High 2.25×.

Maturity factors

Base scenario: Research-stage 0.55× · Developed prototype 0.80× · Developed software 1.00×.

Portfolio expectation

The weighted portfolio-average potential factor and weighted maturity factor are applied to the €60.150M replacement-cost baseline. The resulting base expectation is approximately €78.5M.

Important. These multipliers are transparent portfolio-modelling assumptions, not multipliers prescribed by WIPO, IVSC, RICS, OECD, IFRS, ISO, EPO, JPO or USPTO. Their purpose is to expose strategic upside separately from a cost-based asking reference. During buyer diligence they should be replaced or supplemented with actual market comparables, royalty data, cash-flow forecasts, buyer synergies, real-options analysis and transaction-specific risk inputs.

Reference architecture

Broader than pure code-development cost.

The valuation architecture now draws on core valuation standards, intangible-asset recognition principles, value-creation analysis, IP-management guidance and patent/IP commercialization context.

01

WIPO

IP valuation guidance and the 2025 technology-transfer valuation guide: cost, market, income, real options and Monte Carlo methods.

02

IVS / IVSC

IVS 100–106 for valuation framework, bases, approaches, data, models and reporting, plus IVS 210 for intangible assets.

03

RICS

Professional standard for valuation of intellectual-property rights, expanding on IVS with legal, functional and economic IP characteristics.

04

OECD

Intangibles and DEMPE/value-creation principles used as a check on where economic value is created and controlled.

05

IFRS IAS 38

Identifiability, separability, future-economic-benefit and reliable-measurement concepts for intangible assets including software.

06

ISO 56005

IP-management guidance supporting systematic treatment of IP within innovation and commercialization processes.

07

EPO

Patent-management and valuation guidance covering cost, market and income approaches and future commercial utility.

08

JPO & USPTO

Supplementary institutional context for IP valuation, commercialization, technology transfer and the economic role of intellectual-property assets.

What changes during diligence

A buyer-specific valuation can be substantially different.

WIPO notes that the same IP can have very different value to different buyers. A strategic acquirer may obtain time-to-market savings, failed-project replacement, integration synergies, licensing optionality, defensive value or access to adjacent capabilities that are not captured by reproduction cost alone.

01

Comparable transactions

Introduce actual comparable asset or licensing transactions where reliable data exists.

02

Income model

Build buyer-specific revenues, margins, royalty savings, costs, useful life and risk-adjusted discount rates.

03

Real options

Value the right to continue, defer, expand, license, integrate or abandon as uncertainty resolves.

04

Strategic synergies

Quantify time-to-market, replacement of internal R&D, distribution leverage and other acquirer-specific benefits.

05

Definitive negotiation

Translate value to price only after the exact rights perimeter, risks and transaction structure are known.

Valuation boundary

The model supports negotiation; it does not certify market value.

All published figures are indicative seller-side references. No named institution has reviewed, certified or endorsed a specific asset value. Independent valuation and buyer due diligence remain available as transaction-stage work.
